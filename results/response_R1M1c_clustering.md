# R1, Major 1c — two-way clustering

> Table 1 clusters only by country, while oil price changes and volatility are
> common across countries within a quarter. Report two-way clustered standard
> errors, by country and by quarter.

The referee is right about the structure of the problem, and the consequence is
larger than we expected. The oil price change and the oil volatility series are
single global time series, identical for every country in a given quarter, so
the residuals are correlated across the whole cross-section at each date.
Clustering by country does not address that at all.

We have re-estimated every specification in Table 1 with standard errors
computed four ways. The coefficients are unchanged; only the standard errors
move.

## Oil price volatility

| specification | coefficient | OLS | by country | by quarter | two-way | t, OLS | t, two-way |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no controls, no FE | +0.0158 | 0.0011 | 0.0013 | 0.0063 | 0.0061 | +14.9 | **+2.6** |
| no controls, country FE | +0.0159 | 0.0011 | 0.0013 | 0.0062 | 0.0061 | +14.8 | **+2.6** |
| with controls, no FE | +0.0101 | 0.0015 | 0.0015 | 0.0044 | 0.0044 | +6.8 | **+2.3** |
| with controls and FE | +0.0100 | 0.0015 | 0.0016 | 0.0044 | 0.0044 | +6.6 | **+2.3** |

**The volatility result survives**, but the t statistic falls from 6.6 to 2.3 in
our preferred specification. We will report the two-way standard error, and we
will stop describing this coefficient as precisely estimated.

## The oil price change

| specification | coefficient | OLS | by country | two-way | t, OLS | t, two-way |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no controls, no FE | +0.1880 | 0.0302 | 0.0269 | 0.1293 | +6.2 | **+1.45** |
| no controls, country FE | +0.1844 | 0.0304 | 0.0269 | 0.1299 | +6.1 | **+1.42** |
| with controls, no FE | +0.1415 | 0.0363 | 0.0364 | 0.1505 | +3.9 | **+0.94** |
| with controls and FE | +0.1416 | 0.0366 | 0.0371 | 0.1515 | +3.9 | **+0.93** |

**The oil price change coefficient does not survive.** Under two-way clustering
it is not distinguishable from zero in any specification, with t statistics
between 0.93 and 1.45. We report this rather than bury it.

## What this says about the clustering choice

Clustering by country alone is almost identical to not clustering at all here:
on the volatility coefficient it moves the standard error from 0.0011 to 0.0013.
All of the correction comes from the quarter dimension, which multiplies it by
between 2.9 and 5.8. That is exactly the pattern the referee's reasoning
predicts, and it is why clustering by country was the wrong choice rather than
merely an incomplete one.

## Consequences for the rest of the paper

Two places in the revision depend on this.

**The magnitude comparison.** Referee 2's Major 6 observes that the model's
spread response is far smaller than the data's, using the Table 1 oil price
coefficients of 0.14 to 0.19 to imply 6 to 8 basis points per 10% oil move
against roughly 0.5 to 1.5 in the model. Those coefficients are the ones that
lose significance here. The gap between model and data is therefore smaller than
it appears, and part of it is the imprecision of the data estimate rather than a
shortfall in the model. We make that point in our reply to Major 6 and do not
use it to avoid the question.

**What the paper leads with.** The volatility coefficient survives and the price
coefficient does not, which happens to favour the paper's own emphasis, since
the paper is about volatility. We note that this is what the data say rather
than a choice we made.
